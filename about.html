Who we are

Sihat Bersama is a student initiative based in Bukit Jalil, Kuala Lumpur. We build and maintain this portal as an open health education resource for the Malaysian public. The site is free to use, carries no advertising, and collects no personal data beyond what you choose to send us through the contact form.

The team works with public guidance issued by the Ministry of Health Malaysia and the World Health Organization, rewriting it in plain language so that readers without a medical background can follow it.

Purpose of this portal

To educate people about public health and disease prevention. Every page answers three questions: what the problem is, what causes it, and what a reader can do about it this week.

Mission and vision

Mission

To promote healthier communities by preventing diseases, raising public health awareness, and providing accessible information and resources that empower individuals to make informed decisions about their health.

Vision

To create a healthier world where everyone has the knowledge, resources and support needed to prevent diseases, maintain good health, and live a longer, healthier life.

Background

Public health plays an important role in protecting communities from disease and improving quality of life. This portal was created to provide simple and reliable information on disease prevention, healthy lifestyles, hygiene, vaccination and early detection in one place.

The idea began with a campus project on dengue. While preparing it, we found that most of the material available online was either written for clinicians or repeated advice with no source behind it. We decided to keep the work going after the semester ended and expand it to the other health issues Malaysian families deal with most often. By raising awareness and encouraging preventive action, we aim to help individuals and communities stay healthier and reduce the spread of disease.

How we check our content

  1. A topic is drafted from named public sources, usually Ministry of Health or World Health Organization material.
  2. A second team member checks every claim against those sources before publishing.
  3. The page lists its references at the bottom and carries a review date.
  4. Every page is reviewed again each semester, or sooner if official guidance changes.
  5. Reported mistakes are checked within a few working days and corrections are noted on the page.

Found something wrong?

Tell us through the contact form and point to the paragraph. Corrections are welcome and we credit readers who report them.
