Common health problems in Malaysia

What causes them, and the action that makes the biggest difference.

Summary of frequently reported conditions. Refer to a clinic for diagnosis and treatment.
ConditionTypeMain causeWhat to do
Dengue Mosquito-borne Aedes mosquitoes breeding in clean, still water around the house. Empty and scrub water containers weekly, cover tanks, use repellent, see a doctor early if fever comes with body aches or rash.
Type 2 diabetes Non-communicable Sweet drinks, refined carbohydrates, low activity and extra weight around the waist. Cut sugared drinks first, walk 30 minutes most days, ask for a blood sugar test if there is family history.
High blood pressure Non-communicable Salty and processed food, low activity, poor sleep and long-term stress. Taste food before adding salt or sauce, cook at home more often, check your reading at least once a year.
Obesity Risk factor Large portions of fried and sweetened food, late-night eating, long hours seated. Change one meal a day rather than the whole diet, drink plain water, build movement into the daily routine.
Tuberculosis Airborne, curable Bacteria spread in crowded, poorly ventilated spaces, often ignored as a long cough. Get tested if a cough lasts more than two weeks, keep rooms ventilated, complete the full treatment course.
Hand, foot and mouth disease Viral, children Virus passed through saliva, unwashed hands and shared toys in nurseries and kindergartens. Wash hands with soap, disinfect toys and surfaces, keep a sick child at home until recovered.

This portal does not replace medical care

All content here is general health education. If you have symptoms that worry you, go to the nearest clinic or hospital and speak to a medical professional.

Why this portal exists

Public health protects whole communities rather than treating one patient at a time. Clean water, handwashing, vaccination and early screening prevent far more illness than any medicine taken after the fact.

Most health information online is either too technical or plainly wrong. We collect what matters, rewrite it in plain language, and point readers to the right service when professional care is needed.

Who we are

Sihat Bersama is a student initiative based in Bukit Jalil, Kuala Lumpur. We build and maintain this portal as an open health education resource for the Malaysian public. The site is free to use, carries no advertising, and collects no personal data beyond what you choose to send us through the contact form.

The team works with public guidance issued by the Ministry of Health Malaysia and the World Health Organization, rewriting it in plain language so that readers without a medical background can follow it.

Purpose of this portal

To educate people about public health and disease prevention. Every page answers three questions: what the problem is, what causes it, and what a reader can do about it this week.

Mission and vision

Mission

To promote healthier communities by preventing diseases, raising public health awareness, and providing accessible information and resources that empower individuals to make informed decisions about their health.

Vision

To create a healthier world where everyone has the knowledge, resources and support needed to prevent diseases, maintain good health, and live a longer, healthier life.

Background

Public health plays an important role in protecting communities from disease and improving quality of life. This portal was created to provide simple and reliable information on disease prevention, healthy lifestyles, hygiene, vaccination and early detection in one place.

The idea began with a campus project on dengue. While preparing it, we found that most of the material available online was either written for clinicians or repeated advice with no source behind it. We decided to keep the work going after the semester ended and expand it to the other health issues Malaysian families deal with most often. By raising awareness and encouraging preventive action, we aim to help individuals and communities stay healthier and reduce the spread of disease.

How we check our content

  1. A topic is drafted from named public sources, usually Ministry of Health or World Health Organization material.
  2. A second team member checks every claim against those sources before publishing.
  3. The page lists its references at the bottom and carries a review date.
  4. Every page is reviewed again each semester, or sooner if official guidance changes.
  5. Reported mistakes are checked within a few working days and corrections are noted on the page.

Disease prevention

Information on the diseases reported most often in Malaysia and the actions that reduce their spread. Each guide covers the cause, how it spreads, warning signs, and prevention at home.

Covered topics

  • Dengue and Aedes control around the house
  • Tuberculosis and long-lasting coughs
  • Hand, foot and mouth disease in nurseries and kindergartens
  • Influenza and other respiratory infections
  • Food and water-borne illness, including food poisoning

Benefit

Readers learn which single action matters most for each disease instead of a long list they will not follow.

Health and hygiene tips

Everyday practices that break the chain of infection: handwashing with soap at the right moments, safe food handling and storage, clean drinking water, toilet and bathroom hygiene, and keeping shared spaces ventilated.

Benefit

These habits cost nothing and prevent a large share of common infections, especially in households with young children or older adults.

Vaccination awareness

Plain explanations of how vaccines train the immune system, what the childhood immunisation schedule covers, which vaccines adults may still need, and what the common side effects look like.

Benefit

Parents and adults get answers to the questions that usually cause hesitation, with links to the official schedule rather than rumours from social media.

Healthy lifestyle

Nutrition, physical activity, sleep and stress, written around local food and local routines. Practical swaps for hawker meals, portion guidance, simple exercise that needs no gym, and sleep habits for shift workers.

Benefit

Small, realistic changes that lower the risk of diabetes, high blood pressure and heart disease over time.

Early detection

Warning signs worth acting on, and which screenings to request at each stage of life: blood pressure, blood sugar, cholesterol, body weight, and cancer screening where it applies.

Rule of thumb

A cough lasting more than two weeks, unexplained weight loss, a lump that does not go away, or blood where there should be none are all reasons to see a doctor without waiting.

Health education

Short articles, step-by-step guides and printable resources written for the general public. Teachers may use them in class, and community groups may reproduce them with credit.

Community awareness

Materials and simple activity plans for running an awareness session in a school, office or neighbourhood: poster templates, a talk outline, a checklist for a residential Aedes walkabout, and follow-up ideas.

Invite us to run a session if your group would like help getting started.

Before you start

Medical emergencies

This page answers questions about the portal and general health topics. It is not a triage service. For chest pain, difficulty breathing, heavy bleeding, fits or sudden weakness, call 999 or go to the nearest emergency department immediately.

General

Is this portal a replacement for seeing a doctor?

No. Everything here is general health education. If you have symptoms, or something feels wrong, go to a clinic or hospital and speak to a medical professional. In an emergency call 999.

Do I need to pay or create an account?

No. The portal is free to read and share. There is no sign-up, no paywall and no advertising.

Who runs this website?

A student team based in Kuala Lumpur. Details of the members and their roles are on the About Us page.

Content and sources

Where does your information come from?

Public guidance issued by the Ministry of Health Malaysia and the World Health Organization, rewritten in plain language. References are listed at the bottom of each article.

How often is content updated?

Every page is reviewed at least once a semester, and sooner if official guidance changes. The review date appears on each page.

I found a mistake. How do I report it?

Use the contact form and point to the paragraph in question. Corrections are checked within a few working days and noted on the page.

Using our material

Can I use your content for a school or workplace campaign?

Yes, as long as you credit Sihat Bersama and do not change the meaning of the health advice. Ask us if you need a printable version.

Can you speak at our school, mosque or office?

We run sessions where our schedule allows. Send the date, location and expected audience size through the contact form.

Can we translate your pages?

Yes, with credit. Send us the translated version so we can check that the health advice was not changed during translation.

Privacy

What data does this site collect?

Only what you type into the contact form. We use it to reply to you and nothing else. We do not sell or share it.

Do you use cookies or tracking?

The portal itself does not set tracking cookies. Fonts are loaded from an external provider, which may log the request as any web request is logged.
